Disclosure.

Who writes this, who funds it, and every outside interest that touches the work.

Most research institutions bury their funding and their affiliations. This page does the opposite. It is written in the register of a securities disclosure: complete, flat, and unembarrassed. Where a published document touches one of the interests declared below, the document says so and this page records it. If this page and a paper ever disagree, that is a defect — report it and it will be corrected publicly.

The imprint

The North Pacific Strategy Initiative is written, edited, and funded by one person: Jesse James, of Victoria, British Columbia. There is no staff, no external review board, and no outside funding of any kind. Papers are signed by the author and hosted by the imprint; the institutional register of the site is a discipline of voice, not a claim of institutional scale.

No paid engagement, sponsorship, commission, or compensation of any kind has been accepted — or sought — in connection with this site or any document published on it. No one has commissioned, requested, or directed any NPSI document. If that ever changes, this page changes first.

Declared interests

The editor's outside ventures and interests that touch published subject matter, and the papers each touches. Papers listed here also carry their own notes.

1 · Fit For Gov — civic technology

The editor owns and operates Fit For Gov, a civic-technology practice serving Canadian municipal governments. Touches: Working Paper No. 5, Sovereign Compute North, which was co-issued with Fit For Gov and whose recommendations extend sovereign compute to the municipal layer — a market in which the practice operates. The paper's page carries a co-issuance note. Also adjacent: SB1's municipal sovereign-cloud content.

2 · Enterprise software and applied intelligence

The editor works professionally as a systems architect in enterprise software, data systems, and applied-intelligence platforms. Touches: WP5 and SB1 (sovereign compute and cloud), TB1 (sensing and edge-inference adjacency — TB1 was written independently, with no client and no commission), and the AI-policy subject matter of WP10 and WP11.

3 · Energy facilitation

The editor manages Western operations for an international energy facilitation firm. Touches: WP4, The Addition Paradox (a global energy thesis), and SB3, The Ledger With One Entry (oil-custody analysis). No client of that firm is a subject of, or source for, any NPSI document.

4 · Korean reunification advocacy

The editor separately maintains a public advocacy platform concerning Korean reunification, published under his own name in Korean and English. It is deliberately kept apart from this imprint: NPSI is analytical, not advocacy, and the two are written in different registers for different readers. Touches: the Korea-related papers (WP1, WP3, WP5). Those papers are sourced and falsifiable regardless of the editor's views — that is the standard by which they ask to be judged.

5 · Indigenous standpoint

The editor is of Sagkeeng (Ojibway) ancestry. This is disclosed as standpoint relevant to the Indigenous co-ownership provisions designed into WP1 and WP2 — it is not a claim to speak for any nation or community, and the provisions stand or fall on their design merits.

6 · External writing

The editor writes occasionally for external publications under editorial arrangements that are theirs, not NPSI's. Most recently: a commissioned essay on Korean market structure for Korea Pro (Korea Risk Group), published 29 July 2026. No compensation was arranged as of publication; the editor retained control of the substance, and the piece went through the publication's independent fact-checking process.

7 · Research and drafting assistance

NPSI documents are researched and drafted with substantial assistance from AI systems, principally Claude, made by Anthropic. Where the subject matter makes that assistance material — as in WP10 and WP11, which examine Anthropic's own conduct and statements — the paper carries its own disclosure inset. Editorial responsibility for every published claim rests with the editor.

What the Initiative does not do

No paid engagements. No sponsored research. No advertising, sponsorship, or paywalls. No funder influence — the sole funder is the editor. No document has been commissioned by, written for, or shaped by any client, government, or institution. SB2, The Three Doors, maps public funding programs; no engagement is sought or accepted in connection with the programs it describes. TB1, The Verified Sky, was written on the editor's initiative with no client and no intended customer.

Working-paper numbers are assigned at drafting, and the published sequence carries intentional gaps — Nos. 6 and 8 remain unreleased. No published document has been withdrawn; where a document is superseded (WP10, by WP11), it remains available unaltered with a notice attached. The public git history is the audit trail for both claims.

Corrections

This page is maintained under the same discipline as the papers: versioned in public, corrected in public. If a reader identifies an interest that should be declared here and is not, write to editor@npsi.ca — the correction will be published, not argued with.
