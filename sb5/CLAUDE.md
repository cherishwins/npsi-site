## Special Briefing No. 5 — One Day, Four Names (`sb5/index.html`)

Commemoration-and-record briefing, integrated 4 October 2026 as a **PDF-first release page**. Key facts that should remain consistent:

- **Identity:** the document ID is the one printed on the release, `NPSI-20261003-C` (not `NPSI-SB-005`); on this site it is Special Briefing No. 5. v1.0 issued 3 October 2026; v1.0.1 of 4 October 2026, corrected after a source check, is the release hosted here. In v1.0.1 the "Claims this briefing does not make" box sits at the foot of p. 25, below the essay's close, because the corrected source list filled p. 27.
- **Thesis:** the war in the Pacific ended once; it has at least six official names, and none is neutral; each is a sentence with a subject removed. The line "In Europe they divided the aggressor. In Asia they divided the colony." stays verbatim (editor's decision, 4 October 2026). Two side sentences about Washington were softened in v1.0.1 (pp. 2, 25) under the no-anti-American-framing rule; do not re-harden them.
- **Voice:** the briefing is written in the first person, with passages of opinion marked ("My own view, and it is a view"). The editor kept this; the colophon says so and the release page's interest note says so. The house no-first-person rule is not to be enforced on this document.
- **The names:** Japan — official 戦没者を追悼し平和を祈念する日 (Day for Mourning the War Dead and Praying for Peace, cabinet decision of 13 April 1982), common usage 終戦の日; ROK — 광복절 (statute of 1 October 1949; marks both 1945 and the 1948 founding); DPRK — 조국해방의 날; United States — V-J Day, proclaimed for 2 September 1945 in Truman's radio address of 1 September (Public Papers No. 122); Russia — Day of Victory over Militaristic Japan and the End of the Second World War, 3 September (federal law of 24 June 2023); China — statutory since the NPC Standing Committee decision of 27 February 2014; its Chinese name contains no word for aggression, the official English adds it. None of the six says who drew the line across Korea ("0 of six").
- **The cables (FRUS 1945 vol. VI):** doc 450, Stalin 16 August — "Principally I have no objection against the contents of the order keeping in view that the Liaotung Peninsula is a composite part of Manchuria" (always quote it whole, with the condition), plus the Kurils and the Kushiro–Rumoi line across Hokkaido; doc 452, Truman 17/18 August — the Kurils, with a request for an air base there, but not Hokkaido; doc 470, Stalin 22 August — "in the sense that you refuse to satisfy the request of the Soviet Union"; doc 476, Byrnes's draft of 25 August — the Hokkaido passage "was written at the White House"; doc 771, Rusk's 1950 memorandum — "the night of August 10–11"; doc 776, MacArthur's Proclamation No. 1.
- **The legend:** "two colonels, thirty minutes, a National Geographic map" merges three documents with two dates — FRUS doc 771 (1950), Rusk's memoir (1990; 14 August; quoted at second hand through National Geographic, 2013), and Schnabel (1972; "thirty minutes", "late at night on 10 August"). Fig. 08 superscripts name the document behind each clause.
- **Pyongyang:** Kim Il-sung reached Korea at Wonsan on 19 September 1945, five weeks after liberation; Soviet entry into Pyongyang dated 24 August (some histories give the 25th; open item 09). In 2024, 2025 and 2026 Kim Jong Un laid wreaths at the Liberation Tower. Putin's 2026 message is **Reuters' paraphrase, not a quotation**.
- **1982:** "The headline was wrong. The practice was real." Asahi and others reported on 26 June 1982; Sankei on 28 July; the ministry told the Diet on 30 July it found no such China-front change; Miyazawa's statement of 26 August ("tremendous suffering and damage"); Sankei's correction on 7 September; Asahi's partial apology on 19 September; the Neighbouring Countries Clause in November 1982. Southeast Asia changes and the March First Movement as "riot" were real (Diet record, 23 May 2001).
- **15 August 2026:** Prime Minister Takaichi spoke first and did not say "remorse"; the Emperor spoke of "deep remorse"; "remorse" appears in two of fifteen addresses, 2012 and 2025; four ministers visited Yasukuni (Jiji, Kyodo, NHK); Seoul said it "cannot but deplore" the visits ("deep regret" was the 2025 wording); President Lee proposed talks among the two Koreas and the United States, or those three and China.
- **Declared interest:** the release page carries an interest note for the editor's Korean-reunification advocacy. Describe it; never name or link the platform.
- **Original script:** the briefing's own open item 07 says native-speaker review of its Korean and Japanese lines is pending. Consult the `npsi-korean-translation` skill before adding any further Korean text to the page.
