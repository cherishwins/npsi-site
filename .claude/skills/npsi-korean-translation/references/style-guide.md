## 1. Register — full decision tree

Korean writing has multiple speech levels that are not interchangeable. The four to know:

| Level | Sentence endings | Used for | NPSI use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 한다체 (declarative formal) | -다, -이다, -였다, -한다 | Academic writing, journalism editorials, white papers, government reports | **Working paper body text** |
| 합쇼체 / 합니다체 (deferential formal) | -습니다, -합니다, -입니다 | Business correspondence, formal speeches, professional social media | **LinkedIn captions, formal cover letters** |
| 해요체 (polite informal) | -아요, -어요, -예요 | Casual professional, service interactions, personal blog | **Not used for NPSI** |
| 해라체 (plain informal) | -아, -어, -야 | Personal, intimate, fiction | **Not used for NPSI** |

### Register decision flow

1. **Is this body text inside a working paper?** → 한다체.
2. **Is this a working paper title or section heading?** → Noun phrase, no verb ending. (e.g., "덧셈의 역설" not "덧셈은 역설이다")
3. **Is this a LinkedIn caption, post intro, or anything that directly addresses the reader?** → 합니다체.
4. **Is this a direct quote from a Korean source?** → Mirror the source exactly. Do not normalize.
5. **Is this a chart caption, table title, or figure label inside a working paper?** → Noun phrase or 한다체.
6. **Is this an executive summary or abstract?** → 한다체.

### Common register mistakes

- **Working paper drifting into 합니다체 in the conclusion**: When a writer feels they want the conclusion to "land," they reach for the deferential ending. This breaks register and tells the reader two people wrote the document. Stay in 한다체.
- **LinkedIn caption in 한다체**: This reads as if the post is broadcasting at the audience rather than addressing them. Use 합니다체 for LinkedIn.
- **Mixing 합니다체 and 해요체 within the same paragraph**: Both are polite, but 합니다체 is formal and 해요체 is friendly-casual. They do not co-exist in institutional writing.

## 2. ROK vs DPRK orthography

### Default rule for NPSI

- **NPSI's own voice → ROK orthography**, always. NPSI publishes from Victoria, BC, in the Canadian-Korean institutional context; ROK orthography is the institutional standard.
- **Direct quotes from a DPRK source → DPRK orthography preserved.** Do not "correct" or normalize.

### What differs (orthographic phonology)

ROK and DPRK observe different rules about word-initial consonants:

- **두음법칙** (initial-sound rule): ROK changes word-initial ㄹ to ㄴ (or to ㅇ before high vowels). DPRK preserves the original ㄹ.
  - ROK 노동 / DPRK 로동 (labor)
  - ROK 역사 / DPRK 력사 (history)
  - ROK 이론 / DPRK 리론 (theory)
- ROK drops word-initial ㄴ before /i/, /ya/, /yeo/, /yo/, /yu/. DPRK preserves it.
  - ROK 여성 / DPRK 녀성 (women)
  - ROK 양심 / DPRK 량심 (conscience)
  - ROK 영도자 / DPRK 령도자 (leader)

### Why preserve

A Korean reader at NPS or KIC who sees 로동신문 inside a working paper immediately understands the source is North Korean — that orthographic mark is doing work in the reader's mind. Normalizing it to 노동신문 throws away that signal and reads as either ignorance or a sanitization attempt. Both undermine NPSI's credibility.

### How to handle in formatting

When quoting a DPRK source, set the quote in Korean quote marks 「」 or " ", and preserve the DPRK orthography inside the marks:

> 「위대한 수령 김일성 동지께서는 력사적인 ~을 천명하시었다」

Outside the quote, NPSI returns to ROK orthography immediately. If a single sentence weaves DPRK source language into NPSI commentary, mark the seam clearly with quote characters.

## 3. Numbers, units, and currencies

### Korean numbering base

Korean groups numbers in units of 10,000 (만), not 1,000. Memorize the ladder:

| Power | Korean | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 10⁴ | 만 | man |
| 10⁸ | 억 | eok |
| 10¹² | 조 | jo |
| 10¹⁶ | 경 | gyeong (rare; encyclopedia-only) |

### Conversion practice

| English number | Wrong (calque) | Right (Korean) |
|---|---|---|
| 18.3 million | 18.3백만 | 1,830만 |
| 280 million | 280백만 / 280만 | 2억 8천만 |
| 600 million | 600백만 | 6억 |
| 1 billion | 10억 (NOT 1,000백만) | 10억 |
| 6 trillion | 6,000십억 | 6조 |
| 858 + 314 = 1,172 (WP04 stacked) | 858 더하기 314은 1,172 | 858 + 314 = 1,172 (numerals are fine as digits) |

### Currency notation

| English | Korean |
|---|---|
| USD $1,000 | 1,000달러 (US$1,000 also acceptable on first reference) |
| KRW ₩1,000 | 1,000원 |
| CAD $1,000 | 1,000캐나다달러 / CA$1,000 |
| Euro €1,000 | 1,000유로 |
| JPY ¥1,000 | 1,000엔 |

Place the unit immediately after the number with no space. Korean does not use a space between numeral and Korean unit. (Different from English's "1,000 dollars.")

### Percentages, decimals, and ranges

- 5% → 5% (the % sign is universal)
- 5 percent → 5퍼센트 (spelled-out form is acceptable for emphasis)
- 5.3 → 5.3 (Korean uses the same decimal point)
- 5 to 10 → 5~10 (the tilde is the Korean range mark, NOT a hyphen)
- between 2019 and 2024 → 2019~2024년 / 2019년에서 2024년 사이

### Energy units

| English | Korean |
|---|---|
| TWh | 테라와트시 |
| GWh | 기가와트시 |
| MWh | 메가와트시 |
| kWh | 킬로와트시 |
| barrels (oil) | 배럴 |
| BOE (barrel oil equivalent) | 석유환산배럴 |
| mcf, bcf, tcf (gas) | 천 입방피트, 십억 입방피트, 조 입방피트 — but in practice Korean energy press preserves the English acronym (mcf 등) |

## 4. Punctuation

### Quote marks

- Korean academic and journalistic writing accepts both 「」 (corner brackets) and " " (curly quotes).
- Use one consistently within a document.
- For Sino-Korean technical terms or work titles, 「」 is conventional.
- For dialogue or speech, " " is conventional.
- Single-quote variants 『』 and ' ' nest inside double quotes.

### Sentence-end punctuation

- Period: . (full-width 。 is Japanese — do not use it in Korean)
- Question mark: ?
- Exclamation: ! (use sparingly; almost never in working papers)
- Korean academic writing has very low exclamation density. One exclamation in a 5,000-word paper is a lot. Default to declarative.

### The 가운뎃점 ·

The mid-dot · joins paired nouns that share meaning in compound:
- 한·미 (Korea-US)
- 남·북 (North-South)
- 비핵·평화 (denuclearization and peace)
- 에너지·자원 (energy and resources)

Do NOT use a hyphen for this in Korean. The hyphen is reserved for foreign words and compounds within transliterations.

### Dashes and ranges

- En dash range: 2019~2024 (tilde, not hyphen, not en dash)
- Em dash for parenthetical: — or () — Korean uses parentheses more often than English uses em dashes for asides

### Spacing rules

- Always space between particles and their nouns? **No** — particles attach directly. 한국은 not 한국 은.
- Always space between words? **Yes**, with specific exceptions. 받침 + 의존명사 (bound noun) takes a space: 할 수 있다 not 할수있다 (though contraction is common in casual writing). NPSI uses standard spacing.
- Spacing around English words inserted in Korean text: usually a thin space, but no hard rule. Be consistent within document.
- Spacing inside numerals: Korean does not space "6조 달러" (6 trillion dollars). Number + Korean unit is closed. Number + English unit (USD, KRW) varies; "6조 USD" without space is fine.

## 5. Mixed-language formatting

### English acronyms in Korean text

Convention: gloss the Korean form on first reference, then use the acronym.

> 국민연금공단(NPS)은 ~. 이후 NPS는 ~을 운용하고 있다.

After the first gloss, the bare acronym is correct Korean institutional writing. Do not re-gloss on every mention.

### English proper nouns mid-sentence

Insert directly:

> 캐나다의 LNG Canada 프로젝트는 ~

No transliteration is necessary for English proper nouns embedded in Korean institutional writing. The reader will read the English. Transliterate only when the English form would be genuinely unfamiliar to a Korean policy reader (rare for major Western institutions).

### Numbers with Korean and English mixed in same paragraph

Be consistent within a paragraph. Either:
- All numbers in digits (preferred for technical/finance text): 6조 달러, 280만 배럴, 1,172테라와트시
- Or all numbers spelled out (rare; ceremonial/literary contexts only)

Do not mix "삼백만 달러" and "5조 달러" in the same paragraph.

### Korean inside English text (KO → EN direction)

When translating KO → EN and a Korean institution or person's name appears, the convention for NPSI's English working papers:
- First reference: full English form with Korean in parentheses — National Pension Service (국민연금공단, NPS)
- Subsequent references: acronym alone — NPS

For person names: romanize using the person's preferred romanization. ROK government officials use revised romanization by default (Ahn Chang-ho, not An Chang-ho). DPRK officials use McCune-Reischauer (Kim Jong Un, not Gim Jeong-eun). When in doubt, mirror the romanization the figure uses in their own English-language statements or what the ROK government uses in its English white papers.

## 6. LinkedIn caption format

LinkedIn is read primarily on mobile by Korean readers. The caption format that performs well in institutional Korean LinkedIn:

1. **Hook line** — one sentence, 합니다체, lands the strongest claim.
2. **Blank line.**
3. **Context — 2–3 sentences**, also 합니다체, gives setup without padding.
4. **Blank line.**
5. **Numbers or evidence — 1–2 sentences** with the key statistic or fact.
6. **Blank line.**
7. **Call to action or close** — direct, declarative, optionally with a one-emoji ceiling.
8. (Optional) one or two hashtags. No more.

### Example skeleton (illustrative — not for publication)

> 한반도 에너지 안보의 다음 장은 캐나다 서부에서 열립니다.
>
> 한국가스공사가 LNG 캐나다 지분 5%를 보유하고 있고, 한화는 알고마 스틸에 투자했으며, 작년에는 ROK 잠수함이 빅토리아에 입항했습니다.
>
> 협력의 인프라는 이미 구축되어 있습니다. 부족한 것은 자본 구조뿐입니다.
>
> NPSI 연구보고서 04호에서 그 구조를 제안합니다.
>
> #한반도 #에너지안보

### What to avoid in Korean LinkedIn

- English-style "hooks" that pose a question to the reader (Korean institutional LinkedIn does not work this way).
- Hashtag clusters (4+ hashtags read as marketing automation, not thought leadership).
- Emoji clusters. One emoji maximum, end-of-post.
- Direct Sutter-voice translation. The Korean version of a Sutter post is a *content equivalent* in Korean institutional voice, not a phrase-by-phrase render.

## 7. Worked examples — EN → KO

### Example 1: working paper body sentence

**English (Sutter-voice source):**
> The math is straightforward. Canada has 858 TWh of installable wind and solar capacity on the Pacific corridor; Korea has 314 TWh of dispatchable nuclear that needs a long-tenor offtake partner. Add them. You get 1,172 TWh of stacked clean power.

**Korean (한다체, working paper body):**
> 계산은 단순하다. 캐나다는 태평양 회랑을 따라 설치 가능한 풍력·태양광 용량이 858테라와트시이며, 한국은 장기 수요처가 필요한 디스패처블 원전 용량이 314테라와트시이다. 합치면 1,172테라와트시의 청정전력이 누적된다.

**What was preserved:** the numbers, the basic claim, the rhetorical structure (statement → ingredients → addition).
**What was changed:** "The math is straightforward" → 계산은 단순하다 (not "수학은 직선적이다" which would be a calque). "Add them. You get" → 합치면 ~ 누적된다 (Korean prefers connective endings over English-style staccato sentences in working paper register). The unit became 테라와트시. Native 누적 chosen over English "stacked" calque.

### Example 2: LinkedIn caption opening

**English:**
> Korea spent 75 years divided. The 5,000 years that came before mattered more.

**Korean (합니다체, LinkedIn):**
> 한반도가 분단된 75년보다, 그 이전의 5,000년이 더 중요합니다.

**What changed:** The two English sentences collapsed into one Korean sentence with a comparative structure. Two short English sentences in a caption hook read as Anglo punch; Korean institutional LinkedIn prefers single-sentence comparative grammar for a hook. The numbers carried over directly. 한반도 specified ("the peninsula") rather than "Korea" because Korean ambiguity between 한국 (ROK) and 한반도 (peninsula) would be felt here.

### Example 3: institutional name on first use

**English:**
> NPS, which manages over $800 billion in assets, has begun internal review of Pacific infrastructure exposure.

**Korean (working paper body, 한다체):**
> 국민연금공단(NPS)은 8,000억 달러 이상의 자산을 운용 중이며, 태평양 인프라 투자 노출에 대한 내부 검토에 착수했다.

**What was applied:** First-reference gloss 국민연금공단(NPS), then NPS subsequently. 800 billion → 8,000억 (correct unit). "Has begun" → 착수했다 (institutional Korean for "initiated/began"; 시작했다 would feel too casual for working paper register).

## 8. Worked examples — KO → EN

### Example 1: KCNA-style DPRK statement

**Korean source (KCNA, DPRK orthography preserved):**
> 조선민주주의인민공화국은 미제국주의자들의 책동에 단호히 대처할 것이며, 위대한 수령의 령도 아래 사회주의 강국 건설을 더욱 힘있게 다그쳐 나갈 것이다.

**English translation:**
> The Democratic People's Republic of Korea will resolutely counter the schemes of the U.S. imperialists, and under the leadership [령도] of the Great Leader will more forcefully advance the construction of a socialist great power.

**Translator notes:**
- "령도" preserved with the DPRK orthography form in brackets after the standard English term. This signals to a careful reader that the source uses DPRK political-religious register.
- "미제국주의자들" rendered as "U.S. imperialists" — the term-of-record. Do not soften to "the United States."
- "강국" rendered "great power" — political-economic term, not "strong country."

### Example 2: Yonhap dispatch

**Korean source (Yonhap, neutral institutional):**
> 국방부는 25일 한미 연합훈련 일정과 관련해 "예정대로 진행할 것"이라고 밝혔다.

**English translation:**
> The Ministry of National Defense stated on the 25th that the ROK-US combined exercises would "proceed as scheduled."

**Notes:**
- Date format "25일" → "on the 25th" (assumes reader has the month from context; if not, specify).
- "한미 연합훈련" → "ROK-US combined exercises" (NOT "joint exercises"; combined ≠ joint in alliance technical usage. Combined = multinational. Joint = multi-service single-nation).
- Quote integrity preserved with quote marks.

### Example 3: Chosun Ilbo editorial fragment

**Korean source (Chosun, conservative editorial):**
> 정부의 안일한 대응이 안보 공백을 키운 측면을 부인하기 어렵다.

**English translation:**
> It is difficult to deny that the government's complacent response has widened a security gap.

**Notes:**
- "안일한" → "complacent" (preserves the editorial's critical posture; do not soften to "relaxed").
- "측면을 부인하기 어렵다" is a characteristic Korean editorial hedge structure ("it is difficult to deny [an aspect]"). The English preserves this; do not flatten to "the government has widened a security gap" (which is more assertive than the source).

## 9. Common ML-translation tells

These patterns mark output as machine-translated to a fluent Korean reader. Avoid in EN → KO:

| Tell | Bad | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Subject overspecification | 그것은 중요한 의미를 가진다 | 이는 중요하다 |
| English passive carried over | 회의가 김 장관에 의해 개최되었다 | 김 장관이 회의를 개최했다 |
| 가지다 + noun calque | 회의를 가지다 / 영향력을 가지다 | 회의를 하다 / 영향력이 있다 |
| English relative-clause carried over | 그 채권, 그것은 양국이 발행하는, 자금을 조달한다 | 양국이 발행하는 채권은 자금을 조달한다 |
| "We" / "us" repetition | 우리는 ~ 우리의 ~ 우리가 | (drop most; Korean tolerates much sparser first-person) |
| Article calque ("the") | 그 정부는, 그 정책은 | 정부는, 해당 정책은 (drop or use 해당) |
| Idiom calque ("the numbers don't lie") | 숫자는 거짓말하지 않는다 | 수치는 분명하다 (or context-specific restatement) |
| Conjunction "and" overused | A 그리고 B 그리고 C | A·B·C / A, B, C 등 (Korean is more comma-and-listing) |

Read drafts aloud (or sub-vocally). Korean prose has rhythm; calques break rhythm. If a sentence "thumps" or hesitates when read aloud, it is probably carrying English structure. Rewrite.
