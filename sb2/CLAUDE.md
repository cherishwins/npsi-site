## Canonical facts

- **Version:** v1.0 released 2 July 2026 (ten-panel PDF, canonical); **reading view v1.0.1, 14 October 2026** — the page carries a dated corrections note listing every change against the PDF, which stands as issued. The page's meta description, JSON-LD (`version` 1.0.1) and the Door Two label changed with it.
- **The frame (v1.0.1):** three doors, of which *only Door One* (DPA Title III) rests on Canada counting as a domestic source (50 U.S.C. §4552(7); Pub. L. 102-558 §132, 1992). Door Two and Door Three do not depend on it. Never restore "the 1992 provision opens all three doors".
- **Door One — DPA Title III:** grants, loans, loan guarantees, purchase commitments; Canadian firms apply as domestic sources on Canadian ground. Receipt: US$20M DoD Title III award to Electra Battery Materials, 19 August 2024, Temiskaming Shores cobalt sulfate refinery.
- **Door Two — Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act §40210 (42 U.S.C. §18743), not "Energy Act of 2020":** DOE grants of up to US$10M for pilot projects that process, recycle or develop critical minerals *in the United States*; U.S. siting is a statutory condition, not a preference; a Canadian firm needs a U.S. site or a U.S. partner that has one; authorization US$100M/yr for FY2021–2024 only.
- **Door Three — EXIM Supply Chain Resiliency Initiative:** financing for *international* projects with long-term offtake contracts with U.S. companies; Canada is a foreign supplier here; offtake is the eligibility test and sets the financing size (not "collateral"); EXIM's charter runs to 31 December 2026 (12 U.S.C. §635f) unless renewed.
- **The 1992 term's 2023 amendment:** FY2024 NDAA §1080 (Pub. L. 118-31, December 2023) added Australia *and the United Kingdom*, for Title III only, as a fallback where U.S. and Canadian firms cannot meet a national-defense need, with a 30-day certification to Congress. Canadian firms need no certificate.
- **MP Materials (10 July 2025):** US$400M convertible preferred + warrants that *position* DoD as the largest shareholder (not "became"); ten-year US$110/kg floor under *NdPr output*; separate ten-year commitment to take every magnet from the 10X plant.
- **The 2022 orders:** ISED, 2 November 2022 — Sinomine (Hong Kong) Rare Metals Resources, Chengze Lithium International, Zangge Mining Investment (Chengdu); "foreign investors" / "three Chinese companies" — state ownership is not established; never "state investors".
- **The pipeline figure:** C$117.1B (Canadian dollars) — NRCan Major Projects Inventory 2024: 138 mining projects *under construction or planned* 2024–2034; 67 (C$72.4B) involve critical minerals (about half by count, 62% by value).
- **Citations:** the credit line "Prompted by: J.S. Hale, 'The Critical Minerals Race Is Heating Up' (Neil Hill Global Strategies, 30 March 2026)" is kept (it was in the PDF); primary sources carry the bracketed citations and the Sources list has URLs; the paragraph on Hale ends before the sentence naming his firm's services.
