- **The MOU (14 Nov 2025, MOTIR English text):** ¶7 funding no sooner than 45 business days after selection; ¶8 US$20 billion in any calendar year; **¶9 quoted whole**: Korea may in its sole discretion elect not to fund, forfeits the Deemed Allocation, is paid on the Revised Allocation until the US has received a Catch-up Amount equal to the Unfunded Amount, and the US "may also" impose tariffs at the rate determined by the President; ¶20 shipbuilding is investment and finance; **¶25 the MOU "does not create legally binding rights and obligations."** The operating agreement that would make the cap and the $200 billion binding was *agreed to be drafted* on 1 October, not signed. Japan's MOU (4 Sept 2025) ¶8 carries the **same refusal clause word for word**; what Japan lacks is the cap, the commercial test and a chair.
- **The finding (open question 3 resolved at release):** the walk-away price is *written* and has two parts, the claw-back of returns and a tariff remedy made slower and reviewable by the February ruling. The first draft's "lower than Washington implies" was **dropped** because the claw-back is quantified; do not restore it.
- **Numbers:** $350bn package = $150bn shipbuilding + $200bn strategic investment; Project Star $22.3bn, 6,472 MW, Encinal, Texas, proceeds $43.3–45.4bn over 20 years; Project Power up to $120bn for six AP1000s and two APR1400s, $30bn cap per pair, host states Ohio, Tennessee, South Carolina and Kentucky; room left **$57.7bn if the nuclear framework is drawn in full** (always carry the qualifier); Alaska LNG 807 miles, 20 MTPA, Glenfarne estimate $44.5–54.5bn (3 June 2026), **16 MTPA is the target and 13 MTPA the signed volume**, KOGAS absent, first LNG exports 2031; Lutnick's share figure is **"just over $50 billion"** (the unquoted >$50B stat stays); the White House official's $54bn was given on **30 September**, not the 29th.

Trump* (No. 24-1287, with No. 25-250), 20 Feb 2026, 6–3, IEEPA does not authorise tariffs; only the **reciprocal part** of the 26 January 25-percent threat ran on IEEPA, autos/lumber/pharma are Section 232, and the 25 percent was **never given effect**; the Section 122 surcharge was **10 percent** throughout (24 Feb–24 Jul; CIT ruled it unlawful 7 May, on appeal); Section 301 forced-labour rate 12.5 percent from 24 July; the overcapacity case opened 17 March is **one of two** live instruments, the other the Section 232 auto rate (15 percent from 1 Nov 2025; notice of 4 Dec 2025, FR 2025-21940).
- **Static tariff illustration:** $122.9bn of 2025 exports × ten points ≈ $12bn a year; four years ≈ **$49bn** (not $48bn). The figure is gross, before pass-through and exemptions, and is not a forecast.
