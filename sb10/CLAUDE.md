## Canonical facts (as released, with the corrections above)

- The rule: 10 U.S.C. § 4872 (Pub. L. 115-232 § 871); the upstream bar on material "mined, refined, or separated" in a covered nation from 1 January 2027 (Pub. L. 116-283 § 844, moved from five to six years by Pub. L. 118-31 § 854, rewritten by Pub. L. 119-60 § 848); molybdenum added at once and gallium and germanium from 18 December 2027 (Pub. L. 119-60 § 844). DFARS 252.225-7052 (May 2024, 89 FR 46816) turns on delivery date; 252.225-7961 (June 2026, class deviation 2026-O0041 Rev. 1, revised 1 July) on award date. COTS exception lost at 50% or more of any covered material by weight from 1 January.
- Executive Order 14415, signed 20 July 2026, 91 FR 46693 (23 July): nonavailability waivers cease from 1 January unless a mitigation plan is accepted; failing to qualify a domestic source "shall not constitute non-availability"; regulations to require indentured bills of materials, supplier vetting, 15-day notice and 45-day corrective plans (not binding until the rules exist; guidance about 16 January). The order does not mention "global" nonavailability, the False Claims Act or debarment. Project Vault ($12 billion), EXIM and DFC channels are exempt (§ 6).
- Congress: H.R. 8800 § 1803 (passed 22 July) would repeal the upstream bar on the earlier of 31 December or enactment; S. 4784 § 816 (reported) would move molybdenum to the gallium and germanium date. Neither is law.
- Beijing: the suspension notice for the October 2025 rare earth controls still reads 10 November (MOFCOM Announcement 70); MOFCOM said on 28 September the arrangement runs to 10 January; the pause on the US gallium and germanium ban lapses 27 November (Announcement 72).
- Five findings: 300 t of about 48,000 t of NdFeB magnets used in the US in 2025 came from domestic sources (capacity on track for 5,000 t by end-2026); China refines more than 99% of dysprosium and terbium; dysprosium oxide outside China about 16.3× the Chinese price in late September (6.8× at end-June; NdPr $106.50 against $108.70); no commercial US tungsten mine since 2015 (APT from a 2025 low of $331 to above $3,000 by April 2026); 10 January.
- Chain (Fig. 02): China 69% of rare earth mining (270,000 of 390,000 t, 2025), 60% of magnet rare earth mining, 91% of separation and refining, 94% of sintered magnets, more than 99% of refined Dy and Tb; gallium 99%, germanium 91%, tungsten 79%, molybdenum 37%, tantalum 3%.
- Defence demand: GAO puts it below 0.1% of world consumption; one analyst estimate 200–300 t NdFeB and 200–400 t SmCo a year (Fig. 03 draws 400 to 700 t at its upper estimate); no official figure is public.
- Heavy rare earth capacity outside China (Fig. 05), t Dy+Tb oxide at year end: 290 (2025, Lynas), 490 (2026, MP 200 t), 630 (2027, Energy Fuels 140 t), 865 (2028, Aclara 235 t), 1,093 (2029, Energy Fuels to 368 t). Company targets after 2025; Lynas's figure is a management statement.
- The promoter's read: Oilprice.com, 25 October 2026, named REalloys as "one company"; its disclosure states the owner holds shares. REalloys Q2 2026: $0.8 million revenue, $36.8 million net loss, $122.4 million cash; no compliant heavy rare earth metal at scale on 1 January by its own filings.
- Grid: NdFeB manufacturing about 8.2 kWh/kg (NETL); a 10,000 t plant about 9 MW average; ERCOT large-load requests 63 → 226 → 410 → 474 GW (Dec 2024 to Jun 2026; 90% data centres); transformers 128 weeks.
- People: about 285 US mining and mineral engineering degrees in 2024 against about 600 a year needed; 14 accredited programmes, from 25; at least 11 Chinese universities offer rare earth degrees, none outside China at undergraduate level.
- Draft check: 38 claims across three ledgers (13 on the law; 12 on industry and money; 13 on energy, people and the chorus of quotes), each with a verdict.
- Scenarios: A, the waiver becomes a plan; B, Congress moves the floor; C, Beijing moves first (10 January). Watch list: 10 Nov, 27 Nov, December conference, 1 Jan, 10 Jan, about 16 Jan, Q4 2026.
